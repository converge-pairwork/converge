# CONVERGE client 0.3.3

Invitations, the display and a bridge that survives a restart. An installed 0.3.2 updates itself
to this release within the hour. It speaks only to a relay of the same release.

## Invitations

- The person who invites places the first call, whoever arrives first: the moment the other
  person joins, their bridge is called, and the call connects by itself. The one who joins waits
  for it and does not dial. Whoever calls pays by default, so the inviter pays for that call.
- The invitation is the check between the two people it connects: its message carries the
  inviter's key (`Inviter key:`), which joining pins (`converge_join` with `inviter_key`, or
  `setup --invite ... --inviter-key`). Neither side is asked to compare a fingerprint.

## A bridge that survives a restart

- If the AI client restarts the bridge in the middle of a call (Codex does, for example), the new
  bridge takes the call over: the same call, the messages that arrived meanwhile, and the
  conversation where it was. While a call is up, its state is kept in `calls/` in the private
  state directory, and removed when the call ends.
- Fixed: after a dropped connection, the next messages sent could be lost.

## The display

- The other side's messages are in a heavier frame than your own, headed with who they are from
  and to. What you told your AI is shown beside the message it sent on it.
- A delayed message says how much sooner it could have arrived, in a block of its own.

## Your AI

- It writes as you, in the first person, and to the other side as "you". It never uses the name
  you gave the other person (the bridge refuses a message that does).
- It restates a short answer ("a", "2", "yes") before acting on it, and offers choices only as
  numbered options.
- Setup asks once whether CONVERGE's tools may run without asking each time
  (`converge-bridge setup --allow-tools`).
